Two EDPB documents, archived together because Chapter 1 cites them for one joint proposition.

**Opinion 22/2024** interprets Art. 28 GDPR, where appropriate in conjunction with Chapter V. Two findings are load-bearing for the thesis: (a) where a processor processes personal data **inside the EEA it may still be faced with third-country law**, and adding contract wording such as *"unless required to do so by law or binding order of a governmental body"* **does not release the processor from its GDPR obligations**; (b) for data transferred outside the EEA the EDPB considers it *unlikely* that such wording alone suffices for Art. 28(3)(a) in conjunction with Chapter V, and the possibility of third-country law impeding compliance must be considered *before* entering the contract.

**Recommendations 01/2020 v2.0** cover supplementary measures. Load-bearing findings: remote access from a third country and storage in a non-EEA cloud are themselves **transfers**; where the importer falls under **FISA § 702**, Art. 46 transfer tools may be relied on only if **supplementary technical** measures make access impossible or ineffective; and **"contractual and organisational measures alone will generally not overcome access"**.

**Why archived (2026-08-16, third-pass thesis review).** Chapter 1 previously asserted that the GDPR "can be satisfied for cloud processing through a data-processing agreement and EU data residency", citing only an Art. 32 entry. That is the weaker version of the thesis's own argument: the supervisory authority's published position is that EU residency does not by itself dispose of third-country access exposure and that contract language cannot cure it.
