Three sources archived together because Chapter 2 §2.2 cites them for one connected set of facts that had previously been sourced to § 14 UStG alone.

**§ 27 Abs. 38 UStG — where the phased obligation actually lives.** § 14 UStG defines what an e-invoice *is* and the duty to issue one; the **transitional dates are in § 27 Abs. 38 UStG**, not in § 14. Verified timeline: since **1 January 2025** every domestic business must be able to *receive* a structured e-invoice, with **no transitional relief** (an email inbox suffices); *issuing* is phased — 2025–2026 optional for everyone, **from 1 January 2027** mandatory for issuers with prior-year total turnover **above €800,000**, **from 1 January 2028** for the remainder. A second stage, a VAT reporting system over registered platforms, is expected from around mid-2030.

**Exemptions that keep unstructured documents in circulation permanently.** Per the chamber's FAQ, the obligation does not reach **Kleinunternehmer**, **Kleinbetragsrechnungen** (§ 33 UStDV) or **Fahrausweise**. This strengthens the thesis's transition-period argument: the mixed inflow of structured, hybrid and unstructured documents is not purely transitional for those classes.

**FeRD profile list — six, not five.** ZUGFeRD defines **five** profiles of increasing completeness (MINIMUM, BASIC WL, BASIC, EN 16931 / COMFORT, EXTENDED) **plus the XRECHNUNG reference profile**, added in ZUGFeRD 2.1.1 in consultation with KoSIT as a sixth. XRECHNUNG is a *reference* profile because its XML requirements are specified by KoSIT rather than FeRD, and it admits CII syntax only. Critically for the thesis's legal-grounding argument: **MINIMUM and BASIC WL carry no line items and are recognised only as booking aids, not as complete invoices under the UStG**; BASIC, EN 16931 and EXTENDED are.

**Why archived (2026-08-16, third-pass thesis review).** Chapter 2 listed five profiles while the project's own synthetic corpus contains an XRECHNUNG-profile fixture, and cited § 14 UStG for dates that live elsewhere.
