Art. 28 DSGVO — *Auftragsverarbeiter* (Processor). Governs the relationship between a
controller and a processor: processing may only occur on a documented contract or other
legal act binding the processor, which must specify subject-matter, duration, nature and
purpose of processing, the categories of data, and the controller's obligations. The
processor may not engage a sub-processor without the controller's prior authorisation,
and must impose equivalent obligations down the chain.

## Why cited in HORUS

Cited in the Chapter 1 motivation as the instrument a firm would ordinarily reach for
when a third party processes client documents — and, in the thesis's argument, as the
instrument that is **necessary but not sufficient** here.

The argument the citation supports: an `Auftragsverarbeitungsvertrag` under Art. 28
discharges a *data-protection* obligation. It does not discharge the **sectoral
professional-secrecy** obligation that binds a `Steuerberater`, `Wirtschaftsprüfer` or
`Anwalt` — § 203 StGB together with § 62a StBerG / § 43e BRAO / § 50a WPO, which impose
their own requirements on engaging "mitwirkende Personen" and attach criminal liability.
A data-protection processing agreement is therefore not the confidentiality instrument,
which is the distinction ADR-071 records as load-bearing for the thesis's premise.

Paired in the same passage with Art. 32 (`dsgvo-art-32.md`, the technical-and-
organisational-measures obligation) and with the EDPB's published position that
contractual wording does not release a processor reachable by third-country law.
