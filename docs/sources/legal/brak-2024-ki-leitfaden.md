The federal bar association's AI guidance, the lawyer-side counterpart to the tax-advisor chamber's FAQ. Four findings are load-bearing for the thesis's premise:

1. **A data-protection agreement is not the instrument.** Client information may be disclosed to an AI provider only under the strict conditions of § 43e BRAO; the guidance treats that as a distinct requirement from data-protection compliance.
2. **Mere possibility of access is enough.** As under § 203 StGB, it does not matter whether the provider actually takes notice of the secret — it suffices that it *could*.
3. **Necessity is the gate, and for general chat models the guidance says it fails.** For language models such as ChatGPT, transmitting mandate secrets is, *at the current state of the art*, **not necessary**, because the tool can be used without them and disclosure carries incalculable client risk. This is a stronger argument for local processing than the thesis currently makes, because it attacks the § 43e Abs. 1 precondition rather than the contract.
4. **Anonymisation is usually insufficient.** Removing names and addresses does not suffice where the mandate is identifiable from context.
