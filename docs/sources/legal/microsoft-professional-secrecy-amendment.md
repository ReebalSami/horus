The one publicly documented professional-secrecy amendment from a major cloud provider. Appears under several names for the same document: *Professional Secrecy Amendment for Germany* (Partner Center), *Datengeheimnis-Zusatzvereinbarung für Deutschland (November 2021)*, and in the document itself *Microsoft Customer Agreement — Zusatzvereinbarung für Berufsgeheimnisträger*.

**Verified content.** Microsoft acknowledges that the customer is subject to legal obligations regarding access to its clients' information and that breach can entail criminal consequences under **§ 203 StGB** (the English text names **§§ 203, 204 StGB**); Microsoft is obliged to maintain confidentiality; it is obliged to obtain knowledge of foreign secrets only to the extent necessary for contract performance, with *"obtaining knowledge"* defined as cognitive, human, content-related perception; and Microsoft must ensure that its personnel and sub-processors are bound to secrecy **at least in Textform** and that sub-processors bind their own personnel accordingly. That set maps directly onto the Abs. 3 requirements of § 62a StBerG / § 43e BRAO / § 50a WPO.

**Two limits, both material and both recorded rather than glossed.** (1) The amendment **expires on the earlier of the agreement's termination or the last day of the 36th calendar month after acceptance**, and contains no renewal clause, while the framework agreement it supplements is open-ended. (2) Published university IT-law assessments of the amendment record objections including that it does not use the term *Geheimnis*, an unclear validity period, and **gaps specifically for AI use**; Microsoft considers the agreement sufficient.

**Comparative position of the other providers (as reviewed 2026-08-16).** Amazon: no publicly linked § 203 standard agreement; the route is an individual agreement. Google: a § 203 addendum exists but is **not publicly available** — requested under NDA and handled on a best-effort basis. Direct model-API providers (OpenAI, Anthropic): data-processing addenda only, silent on professional secrecy.

**Why archived (2026-08-16, third-pass thesis review).** Chapter 1 asserted that "at least one major cloud provider offers a contractual amendment" without citing it, and asserted absolutely that the providers of the strongest general-purpose models "do not currently offer an equivalent amendment" — which is false for Google if the non-public route counts. The corrected passage narrows the claim to what is provable and cites this record. Cited in `thesis/references.bib` as `msprofsecrecy`.
