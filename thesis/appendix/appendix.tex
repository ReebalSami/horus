\chapter{Pre-Registered Hypotheses}
\label{app:hypotheses}

This appendix reproduces the hypothesis register verbatim, with registration dates and
dispositions. The register's canonical home is the project's decision-record trail
(ADR-031, which also documents a label-reconciliation audit that found and corrected
three classes of hypothesis-citation errors --- including one fabricated citation ---
before any hypothesis was reported against). H1--H6 were registered on 2026-05-08,
before the corresponding data collection; H8 was formalised on 2026-05-31, after its
measurement instrumentation existed but before the efficiency test ran, and is
honestly dated as such. Several thresholds were registered symbolically (X, Y, Z, N,
L) with the stated intention of fixing them at test design time; for the hypotheses
that were never tested, they were consequently never fixed, and are reproduced here as
registered.

\begin{description}
  \item[H1 (Layer 1) --- registered 2026-05-08.] Local document \acp{VLM}

        state of the art on \ac{ZUGFeRD} German invoices.
        \emph{Disposition: the local arm is characterised in

  \item[H2 (Layer 1, architecture) --- registered 2026-05-08.] Single-shot
        end-to-end \acp{VLM} beat orchestrated specialist pipelines on \emph{clean}
        \ac{ZUGFeRD}; orchestrated pipelines beat single-shot on \emph{degraded}
        Belege where validator-retry corrects errors (directional flip).
        \emph{Disposition: partially addressed as a preliminary, pre-correction
        study (Section~\ref{sec:results-preliminary}) whose qualitative outcome informed
        the architecture; not confirmatory. The validator-retry arm was never built
        (Section~\ref{sec:unevaluated}).}
  \item[H3 (Layer 2) --- registered 2026-05-08.] Compliance-aware extraction
        \ac{F1} predicts knowledge-graph compliance pass-rate monotonically;
        standard token \ac{F1} is a worse predictor.
        \emph{Disposition: not evaluated --- the layer was designed, not built.}
  \item[H4 (Layer 3) --- registered 2026-05-08.] Graph retrieval beats vector
        retrieval on multi-hop and compliance-check queries by ${\geq}$Y; vector
        matches or exceeds on simple lookups.
        \emph{Disposition: not evaluated --- presupposes the graph.}
  \item[H5 (Layer 3) --- registered 2026-05-08.] A query-feature-only router
        reaches accuracy within Z of an oracle router.
        \emph{Disposition: not evaluated.}
  \item[H6 (conditional) --- registered 2026-05-08.] Validator-driven retry on
        single-shot \acp{VLM} closes the compliance-pass-rate gap to cloud by
        ${\geq}$N~pp within latency budget L.
        \emph{Disposition: not evaluated --- the retry variant was never
        implemented, and no figure in this thesis reflects one.}
  \item[H7 --- floated, never locked.] A transfer-learning / template-shift
        robustness hypothesis was discussed as a candidate and never registered.
        \emph{Disposition: no claim attaches to it. Template shift was partially and
        accidentally addressed by the held-out corpus
        (Section~\ref{sec:unevaluated}).}
  \item[H8 (Layer 1, efficiency) --- formalised 2026-05-31.] \ac{MLX}-routed
        \acp{VLM} achieve ${\geq}3\times$ decode tokens per second compared to
        Transformers-\ac{MPS}-routed \acp{VLM} at comparable \ac{F1} on the
        \ac{ZUGFeRD} corpus, and the cohort's local document \acp{VLM} run within
        the M1~Pro / 16\,\ac{GB} unified-memory envelope. The ${\geq}3\times$
        threshold is literature-grounded, not fitted to any project result.
        \emph{Disposition: partially evaluated by a dedicated eight-model sweep.
        The memory-envelope clause holds for seven of the eight, whose peak working
        sets range 1.31--8.40\,\ac{GB} against the host's 12.71\,\ac{GB}
        recommended ceiling; the eighth breached it at 105\,\% and is documented as
        the exception. The ${\geq}3\times$ decode clause is not cleanly evaluable on
        this hardware --- decode-only timing is not exposed by the
        \ac{MPS} backend's public interface, and the one deliberately
        model-matched and precision-matched pair failed on that backend, so the
        ``at comparable \ac{F1}'' precondition never held. That is a final,
        documented position rather than an open task.}

\chapter{The Abandoned Compliance-Weighted Metric}
\label{app:metric-spec}

This appendix is a design record of something this thesis chose \emph{not} to do, kept
because the abandonment is itself a methodological decision the reader should be able
to audit (Sections~\ref{sec:contributions} and~\ref{sec:method-metrics}).

The original design (registered with the hypothesis set on 2026-05-08) was a
\emph{compliance-aware} \ac{F1}: each registry field would carry a weight of 3, 2
or~1 according to its legal class --- weight~3 for the particulars whose absence
voids input-tax deduction under \S~14 \ac{UStG} \parencite{ustg14}, weight~2 for
particulars required in full invoices but relaxed for small-amount invoices under
\S~33 \ac{UStDV} \parencite{ustdv33}, weight~1 for the remainder --- and the headline
score would weight per-field outcomes accordingly. H1 and H3 were originally phrased
against this metric (Appendix~\ref{app:hypotheses}).

It was abandoned before any result was reported against it, for one reason: the
weight vector would have been chosen by the author. A number of the form
``compliance-weighted \ac{F1} $= 0.91$'' is not comparable to anything outside this
thesis, cannot be checked against any external convention (the statutes rank nothing;
they enumerate), and moves under any re-weighting a critic prefers. Unweighted
figures with full per-field reporting dominate it for every purpose except producing
a flattering single number: a reader who wants a weighted score can compute one from
the published per-field results under weights of their own choosing, and the
legally grounded field \emph{classes} survive in the registry itself
(Appendix~\ref{app:registry}) rather than in the scoring.

What was kept from the design: the field registry's alignment to \ac{EN}~16931
business terms, the per-field reporting that makes any external weighting computable,
and the spurious-emission rate as a first-class metric --- the one part of the
original ``compliance-aware'' intent that survived scrutiny, because inventing a
value for an absent field is an error class the statutes make professionally
meaningful independent of any weight vector.

\chapter{AI-Tool Usage Documentation}
\label{app:ai-usage}

The statutory declaration of this thesis asserts that content created or edited by an
artificial intelligence is marked as such (\emph{``\dots{}durch eine k\"unstliche
Intelligenz wie ChatGPT erstellte oder bearbeitete Inhalte sind als solche kenntlich
gemacht''}). This appendix is that marking: in accordance with the FH Wedel guidance on
AI use in academic work \parencite{fhwedelrichtlinie}, it documents which tools were used,
for which purposes, in which phases, and how their output was verified --- specifically
enough to be auditable.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Tools and purposes.} Agentic large-language-model coding assistants ---
        Windsurf Cascade with Anthropic Claude-family models as the primary assistant,
        and GitHub Copilot for in-editor completion --- were used throughout the project
        for: implementing the experimental harness, evaluation code, scripts and tests
        under continuous author review; drafting and revising the manuscript's prose
        from the author's committed evidence base; literature search assistance; and
        generating the deterministic asset pipeline that renders every table and figure
        from committed measurement artefacts. Separately, two cloud \ac{API} services
        were used for one \emph{data-authoring} purpose only, never on the inference
        path: a frontier vision model and a commercial document-understanding service
        as two of the three adjudication channels for the held-out answer key
        (Section~\ref{sec:method-heldout-gt}), with the privacy trade-off stated there.
  \item \textbf{Code.} The experimental harness, evaluation code, scripts and tests
        were developed with AI assistance under continuous author review, in a
        repository whose every change passed through version control, linting,
        type-checking and the test suite (Section~\ref{sec:impl-verification}). All
        architectural and methodological decisions are recorded as numbered decision
        records with their rationale (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).
  \item \textbf{Prose.} Chapters were drafted iteratively with AI assistance from
        the author's own evidence base --- the committed measurement artefacts,
        decision records and audit documents --- and revised and approved by the
        author, who takes full responsibility for every claim. Multiple review
        passes, documented under \texttt{docs/reviews/} in the repository ---
        including one adversarial re-audit of a prior pass --- checked the
        manuscript's claims against the committed evidence and the cited sources.
  \item \textbf{What AI did not produce.} No experimental result, measurement,
        or reported number was generated by a language model: every number in this
        manuscript is emitted by the deterministic asset pipeline from committed
        measurement artefacts (Section~\ref{sec:impl-assets}), and the measurements
        themselves come from the instrumented runs described in
        Chapters~\ref{ch:methodology} and~\ref{ch:results}. Literature citations
        were verified against the cited sources; the bibliography records
        per-entry verification notes.
  \item \textbf{Verification practice.} AI-assisted output was treated as untrusted
        input: reviewed, tested, and cross-checked against primary sources before
        adoption. Defects found during those checks --- including in AI-drafted
        text and citations --- are documented in the repository's review records.
\end{itemize}

Responsibility for the correctness of the content, and for the selection and assessment
of every AI-assisted result, lies fully with the author. Every claim, number and citation
in this manuscript was reviewed and approved by the author, and the author signs the
statutory declaration on that basis.

\chapter{Reproducibility and Decision Records}
\label{app:repro}

Everything reported in this thesis is reproducible from one repository, and the
reasoning trail is part of the repository rather than of the author's memory.

\begin{description}
  \item[Repository layout.] Source under \texttt{src/horus/}; experiments as
        jupytext-paired scripts under \texttt{experiments/}; configuration as
        validated \ac{YAML} under \texttt{configs/}; evaluation evidence under
        \texttt{eval/} and \texttt{data/finetune/}; the manuscript under
        \texttt{thesis/}. Private held-out data lives in a git-ignored tree and is
        represented publicly by the sanitised datasheet of
        Appendix~\ref{app:heldout}.
  \item[Decision records.] Every tool, model, library, dataset and methodological
        choice is recorded as a numbered architecture decision record
        (\texttt{docs/decisions/}, indexed) stating context, options, decision and
        supersession trigger. The hypothesis register and its label-reconciliation
        audit live in the same trail (Appendix~\ref{app:hypotheses}).
  \item[Environment.] Python version and dependencies are pinned (lockfile);
        the package manager is uv; local inference uses \ac{MLX}
        \parencite{mlxvlm} on the hardware stated in Section~\ref{sec:method-repro};
        adaptation runs used the recorded rented-\ac{GPU} environment with
        per-run provenance files capturing the recipe, seeds, library versions and
        loss curves (Section~\ref{sec:results-adaptation}).
  \item[Experiment tracking.] Runs are tracked in MLflow with their configuration
        and metrics; sealed splits are fixed by recorded hashes
        (Section~\ref{sec:method-splits}); every generation --- transcripts, structurer
        outputs, scores --- is persisted, which is what makes the frozen-generation
        re-scoring of Chapter~\ref{ch:measurement-validity} possible.
  \item[Manuscript build.] The thesis builds with \texttt{make thesis}
        (latexmk); every table and figure is generated by
        \texttt{make thesis-assets} from committed evidence and included verbatim
        (Section~\ref{sec:impl-assets}). No measured number in this manuscript is typed
        by hand.
  \item[Verification gates.] \texttt{make test} (the suite whose size
        Chapter~\ref{ch:implementation} reports), \texttt{make lint},
        \texttt{make typecheck}; continuous integration runs on every push to the
        main branch and every pull request against it.
\end{description}
