German tax advisors, auditors and lawyers process client invoices under a
professional-secrecy regime backed by criminal law, which makes sending those
documents to external cloud services legally fraught and motivates processing
that never leaves the firm. This thesis asks how reliably current open-weights,
locally hosted document \acp{VLM} can convert German business-to-business
invoices into the legally grounded \ac{EN}~16931 semantic form on commodity
hardware --- and which stage of the pipeline limits that reliability. A
two-stage pipeline is built and measured: a vision-language model transcribes
the page, and a second model structures the transcript against the field
registry, both running locally. Measurement rests on a synthetic \ac{ZUGFeRD}
corpus whose embedded \ac{XML} supplies machine-exact references, and on 39 real
invoices with an answer key adjudicated across three independent channels.

Zero-shot header-field extraction on the real invoices reaches a mean
per-invoice \ac{F1} of 0.88, with precision (0.95) well above recall (0.85):
four errors in five are omissions, not fabrications. Capture quality, not
language, is the dominant cost --- phone-scanned documents score more than
eleven points below email-native ones. Because the structuring stage exceeds
0.97 on perfect transcripts, the binding constraint is reading, a conclusion
supported by a per-miss attribution of every individual error. A pre-registered
low-rank adaptation of the structuring stage regresses in all four cells of its
two-by-two design and specifically raises the rate of values invented for absent
fields.

A recurring finding is methodological: the measuring apparatus itself required
validation. Documented defects in scoring rules and reference data moved
reported figures by amounts comparable to the differences between models and
once inverted a model ordering; the thesis contributes the defect taxonomy, the
frozen-generation re-scoring discipline that makes instrument repairs measurable
in isolation, and the resulting corrected results. A knowledge-graph layer and a
query layer were designed but deliberately not built or evaluated; their
hypotheses are reported as registered but unevaluated.
