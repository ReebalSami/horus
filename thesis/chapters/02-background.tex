\chapter{Background}
\label{ch:background}

This chapter supplies the foundations the rest of the thesis assumes: how a
vision-language model reads a page, what a German electronic invoice is and why it
carries its own reference data, the minimum of knowledge-graph vocabulary needed to
follow the design of the unbuilt layers, and the evaluation concepts the metric suite is
built from. It is tutorial by intent; the positioning against prior work follows in
Chapter~\ref{ch:related-work}.

\section{OCR-Free Document Understanding and Vision-Language Models}
\label{sec:bg-vlm}

\subsection{How a vision-language model reads a page}

Every model in this thesis is a transformer \parencite{vaswani2017attention}: a sequence
model whose attention layers let each token condition on every other. Vision
transformers extend the same machinery to images by slicing them into fixed-size
patches, embedding each patch as a token, and processing the resulting sequence exactly
like text \parencite{dosovitskiy2021vit}. A \ac{VLM} combines the two: a vision encoder
turns the page image into a sequence of visual embeddings, a small projection maps them
into the language model's embedding space, and an autoregressive decoder then generates
text conditioned on both the visual tokens and a textual prompt. Figure~\ref{fig:vlm-anatomy}
shows the anatomy. Nothing in that
architecture knows what an invoice is. Everything this thesis measures --- reading
quality, structuring quality, abstention behaviour --- is behaviour learned from
training data, elicited through prompts, and constrained after the fact by deterministic
validation (Section~\ref{sec:design-validation}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \input{figures/vlm-anatomy.tex}
  \caption[How a vision-language model reads a page]{How a vision-language model reads a
  page: the image becomes a sequence of patch tokens, a vision encoder contextualises
  them, a projection maps them into the language model's embedding space, and an
  autoregressive decoder generates text conditioned on the visual tokens and the prompt.
  No component is invoice-specific.}
  \label{fig:vlm-anatomy}
\end{figure}

The classical alternative is an \ac{OCR} pipeline: detect text regions, recognise the
characters in each, then reassemble. Its known weakness is that recognition and layout
reconstruction are separate problems, and errors in the second silently corrupt the
first's output ordering. Document understanding research therefore moved first to models
that consume \ac{OCR} output together with layout coordinates
\parencite{huang2022layoutlmv3}, and then to \emph{\ac{OCR}-free} models that map pixels to
output directly, with Donut as the canonical demonstration \parencite{kim2022donut}. The
models this thesis surveys are \ac{OCR}-free in that sense: no external text detector or
recogniser runs anywhere in the pipeline.

A note on names, because they invite the opposite conclusion. Several models evaluated here
carry \ac{OCR} in their names --- olmOCR-2, DeepSeek-OCR, PaddleOCR-VL --- and one might read
that as contradicting the claim just made. It does not. The names denote the \emph{task} the
model was specialised for, page transcription, which is historically \ac{OCR}'s task. They do
not denote the \emph{mechanism}: each is a single vision-language model that maps pixels to
text in one pass, with no character-recognition engine inside it and none invoked beside it.
\ac{OCR}-free is a claim about pipeline architecture throughout this thesis, never about
nomenclature.

Document-specialised \acp{VLM} share that anatomy and differ in scale, in specialisation
and in what they emit --- Figure~\ref{fig:cohort-comparison} contrasts the three families
this thesis draws on. granite-docling, a 258-million-parameter model built on the Idefics3
architecture, emits DocTags,
a compact structural markup designed for downstream conversion \parencite{granitedocling,
docling2025}; olmOCR-2, a transcription-specialised fine-tune of the general-purpose
Qwen2.5-VL-7B, emits naturally ordered plain text optimised for faithful
transcription \parencite{olmocr2}; general-purpose models such as Qwen3-VL emit whatever the
prompt asks, including \ac{JSON} \parencite{qwen3vl}. This thesis's pipeline deliberately
asks its reading model for plain text and its structuring model for \ac{JSON}, keeping
the two output disciplines apart; Section~\ref{sec:design-two-stage} gives the reasons.

\subsection{Running such models locally}
\label{sec:bg-local}

The privacy premise of Chapter~\ref{ch:introduction} confines every deployed model to
hardware a company plausibly owns on premises --- concretely a workstation with a single modern
24\,\ac{GB} graphics processor as the target class, and a laptop with 16\,\ac{GB} of unified
memory as the measured floor (Section~\ref{sec:method-repro}). Two techniques make that envelope
workable, and both appear throughout the results.

\textbf{Quantisation} stores the trained weights at reduced numeric precision. The
models here run in 4-bit quantised form for deployment, roughly a quarter of the weight
storage of the 16-bit (\ac{bf16}) representation they were trained in --- weight storage
only, since activations, attention caches and image preprocessing consume memory a
quantised checkpoint does not reduce. Quantisation is not
free: Chapter~\ref{ch:results} measures the cost directly, and
Chapter~\ref{ch:measurement-validity} shows how comparing numbers across precision
regimes without re-measuring the baseline very nearly inverted a conclusion. Local
execution on Apple hardware uses \ac{MLX} and its \ac{VLM} runtime
\parencite{mlxvlm}.

\textbf{Parameter-efficient adaptation} changes a model without retraining it. \ac{LoRA}
freezes the base weights $W$ and trains a low-rank additive update $\Delta W = BA$,
where $B$ and $A$ have inner rank far smaller than the weight matrix's dimensions
\parencite{hu2021lora}, as Figure~\ref{fig:lora-update} sketches. Only $A$ and $B$ are
trained and stored, which is what makes
adaptation affordable on a single graphics processor and distributable as a small artefact.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \input{figures/lora-update.tex}
  \caption[Low-rank adaptation]{Low-rank adaptation \parencite{hu2021lora}: the pretrained
  weights $W$ stay frozen while a low-rank additive update $BA$ is trained beside them;
  the two paths sum at the output. The thinness of $A$ and $B$ is the method --- the
  update has rank at most $r$.}
  \label{fig:lora-update}
\end{figure}

What such an update can and cannot teach a model is an empirical question with a growing
literature (Section~\ref{sec:rw-adaptation}), and this thesis's adaptation experiment
(Section~\ref{sec:results-adaptation}) is a data point in it.

All reported generations are \emph{greedy}: the decoder always takes its
highest-probability token, so a given model, prompt and input produce the same output
every time. The measurement consequences of that choice are discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:method-architecture} and its threats in Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion}.

\section{German Electronic Invoicing: ZUGFeRD, XRechnung, EN~16931}
\label{sec:bg-einvoicing}

\subsection{The semantic model and its carriers}

\ac{EN}~16931 is the European standard defining the \emph{semantic model} of an
electronic invoice: an enumerated set of business terms with defined meanings,
cardinalities and datatypes \parencite{en16931} --- \ac{BT}-numbered fields such as BT-1
(invoice number), BT-31 (seller \ac{VAT} identifier) and BT-112 (grand total
including \ac{VAT}). The
standard deliberately separates \emph{what} an invoice says from \emph{how} it is
serialised. Germany carries the model in two forms. \textbf{XRechnung} is the national
pure-\ac{XML} profile for invoices to public-sector buyers, maintained by KoSIT
\parencite{xrechnung}: machine-first, with no visual page at all. \textbf{\ac{ZUGFeRD}}
(technically aligned with the Franco-German Factur-X specification) is a \emph{hybrid}:
a \ac{PDF}/A-3 file whose visible pages are an ordinary human-readable invoice and which
embeds, inside the same file, a machine-readable \ac{XML} instance of the invoice in
\ac{CII} syntax \parencite{zugferdanhang}. \ac{ZUGFeRD} defines five conformance profiles
of increasing completeness --- MINIMUM, BASIC~WL, BASIC, EN~16931 (also called COMFORT),
EXTENDED --- plus a sixth, XRECHNUNG, which is a \emph{reference} profile whose \ac{XML}
requirements are set by KoSIT rather than by FeRD, so that a hybrid file can satisfy the
public-sector standard \parencite{ferdprofiles}. The profile fixes how much of the semantic
model the embedded \ac{XML} must carry, and two of the six carry too little to be invoices
at all: MINIMUM and BASIC~WL omit line items and are recognised only as booking aids, not
as complete invoices under German \ac{VAT} law \parencite{ferdprofiles}. That matters for
this thesis twice over: the corpus of Section~\ref{sec:method-data} deliberately spans the
range including those two profiles, and Section~\ref{sec:bg-legal-target} argues that the
extraction target is legally grounded, which is a claim about the \emph{field definitions}
rather than about every document in the corpus being a lawful invoice.

Electronic invoicing is no longer optional in Germany. Since January~2025 every domestic
business must be able to \emph{receive} a structured electronic invoice, with no
transitional relief \parencite{ustg14}; the duty to \emph{issue} one is phased in
separately, from 2027 for issuers above a turnover threshold and from 2028 for the rest
\parencite{ustg27,bstbkerechnung}. Two exemptions keep unstructured documents in
circulation regardless: small-amount invoices and small-business issuers are outside the
obligation altogether \parencite{bstbkerechnung}. The professions this thesis targets
therefore sit in a transition period --- and, for the exempt classes, a permanent state ---
in which structured invoices, hybrid invoices and entirely unstructured paper or
\ac{PDF} documents arrive side by side, which is exactly the situation the
held-out corpus of Section~\ref{sec:method-data} samples.

\subsection{Why the extraction target is legally grounded}
\label{sec:bg-legal-target}

German law prescribes invoice content independently of any serialisation standard.
\S~14 \ac{UStG} lists the mandatory particulars an invoice must carry: the parties'
identities, the supplier's tax or \ac{VAT} identifier, issue date, a sequential invoice
number, the supply, the net amount per rate, the tax rate and amount \parencite{ustg14}.
\S~33 \ac{UStDV} relaxes those requirements for small-amount invoices up to
250~Euro, which may lawfully omit the recipient's identity and the separate tax amount
\parencite{ustdv33}. Two consequences matter for this thesis. First, the field registry
(Section~\ref{sec:method-schema}) is an alignment to \ac{EN}~16931 business terms rather than
an invented schema, so an extracted field's meaning is fixed by the standard rather than by
this thesis, with one documented exception: a single document-level tax rate that the
standard defines only per breakdown group and which is therefore ill-posed on multi-rate
invoices (Section~\ref{sec:validity-reference}). Second, field
\emph{absence} is frequently lawful rather than defective --- a Kleinbetragsrechnung
simply does not carry a buyer identity --- which is one root of the honest-null contract
(Section~\ref{sec:design-honest-null}): a system that guesses a value for a field the document
lawfully omits is wrong in a way that matters professionally.

\subsection{The embedded reference as free ground truth}

The hybrid carrier has a property this thesis's method depends on: \emph{the same file
contains the system's input and the evaluation's reference}. Rendering the visible pages
of a \ac{ZUGFeRD} document yields the page images a \ac{VLM} pipeline consumes;
extracting the embedded \ac{CII} \ac{XML} yields a machine-readable record of the same
invoice, produced by the issuer's own software rather than by an annotator.
Ground truth obtained this way is free of annotation cost and of annotator fatigue, and
it is exact in the narrow sense that matters most: it is not one person's reading of the
page. It is available only for documents that carry the embedding, which born-digital
synthetic corpora do and real scanned paper does not --- an asymmetry that forces the split
corpus design of Section~\ref{sec:method-data}.

Two warnings belong here rather than later, because the method reads as stronger than it
is without them. First, the embedded record states what the issuer's software
\emph{encoded}, which is not by definition identical to what the rendered page
\emph{shows}; the two can diverge, and where they do the reference can be an unfair
standard for a system that is only allowed to look at the page. Second, exactness and
fairness are different properties: the record stores canonical machine values while the
page prints human-readable ones, so a comparison can penalise a correct reading purely on
representation. Chapter~\ref{ch:measurement-validity} reports what both warnings cost in
practice --- reference data that was wrong or ill-posed, and comparison rules that punished
correct answers --- and it is the reason that chapter exists at all.

\section{Knowledge Graphs and Graph-Augmented Retrieval}
\label{sec:bg-kg}

This section is deliberately brief: the layers it supports were designed but not built
(Section~\ref{sec:design-scope}), so the reader needs vocabulary, not a survey.

A \ac{KG} represents entities as typed nodes and relations as typed edges --- for this
domain, a supplier node connected to invoice nodes, which connect to line items,
accounts and periods. Building one from documents requires \emph{entity resolution}:
deciding that ``M\"uller GmbH'' on one invoice and ``Mueller GmbH \& Co.\ KG'' on
another are, or are not, the same supplier. Resolution errors change the graph's
structure, not just its labels, which is why graph construction has to be evaluated at
both the edge level and the structure level \parencite{cai2025}.

The retrieval side motivates why one would want such a graph at all.
\ac{RAG} answers questions by retrieving text chunks and generating over them;
graph-augmented retrieval (GraphRAG) retrieves over the extracted graph instead. It is
proposed for exactly the query shapes flat retrieval handles poorly --- aggregations
(``total spend with this supplier this quarter'') and multi-hop connections --- at the cost
of building and maintaining the graph; whether it delivers that advantage is an active and
unsettled question rather than a settled result \parencite{edge2024graphrag,han2025}.
The intended Layer~2/3 design (Section~\ref{sec:design-layer2},
Section~\ref{sec:design-layer3}) and the hypotheses registered for it
(Chapter~\ref{ch:limitations-future-work}) rest on this vocabulary and on nothing more
advanced than it.

\section{Evaluation Foundations}
\label{sec:bg-eval}

\subsection{Precision, recall and F$_1$ over extracted fields}

Comparing one extracted field against its reference yields one of \emph{five} outcomes,
and all five are needed to follow the later chapters. A \ac{TP} is a field whose extracted
value matches the reference; an \ac{FP} is an emitted value that is wrong or belongs to a
field the document does not carry; an \ac{FN} is a reference value the system failed to
produce; a \ac{TN} is a field the document does not carry and the system correctly left
empty; and a fifth outcome, \emph{excluded}, marks a cell that is ill-posed and is scored
neither way --- a document-level tax rate on an invoice carrying two different rates, for
instance. Section~\ref{sec:method-metrics} states the exclusion discipline that keeps that fifth
category from becoming a way to improve a score, and
Chapter~\ref{ch:measurement-validity} reports every exclusion actually applied.

The first three counts give the familiar measures. Precision
$P = \mathit{TP}/(\mathit{TP}+\mathit{FP})$ measures how trustworthy
an emitted value is; recall $R = \mathit{TP}/(\mathit{TP}+\mathit{FN})$ measures how
much of the document's content was recovered; \ac{F1} $= 2PR/(P+R)$ is their harmonic
mean, dominated by whichever is worse. Correct absences do not enter that family, because
including them would make every score partly a function of how often a corpus happens to
leave fields empty. They are not discarded either: the rate at which the system emits a
value for a field the document does not carry --- spurious emission, $\mathit{FP}/(\mathit{FP}+\mathit{TN})$ ---
is exactly what the \ac{TN} count is for, and it is tracked as a metric in its own right.
That asymmetry between the two error directions is
not cosmetic in this domain: an \ac{FN} is a visible blank a reviewer will fill, while
an \ac{FP} is a plausible-looking wrong answer, which is why
Chapter~\ref{ch:results} reports the error \emph{profile} and the spurious-emission rate
alongside \ac{F1}.

Aggregation is a second design decision. \emph{Micro-pooled} scores add the counts over
all documents before computing \ac{F1}, weighting every cell equally; a \emph{mean
per-invoice} score computes \ac{F1} per document and averages, weighting every document
equally regardless of how many fields it carries. The two answer different questions ---
cell-level accuracy against document-level experience --- and diverge when documents
vary in field count, so the results chapters report both and say which is which.
Matching itself (when is an extracted string ``equal'' to the reference?) is a third
decision with its own literature and its own defect history; Section~\ref{sec:rw-evaluation}
positions it and Chapter~\ref{ch:measurement-validity} audits it.

\subsection{Pre-registration and HARKing}

A hypothesis stated after the data has been seen is not a test, it is a description.
Kerr \parencite{kerr1998} named the practice \ac{HARKing} --- hypothesising after the
results are known --- and catalogued why it corrupts the evidentiary value of
research even when every individual number is correct. The standard defence is to register
hypotheses, decision rules and thresholds \emph{before} the corresponding data is
collected, so that an outcome can be checked against a commitment instead of a memory.
This thesis registers its hypothesis set and selection rules in advance
(Section~\ref{sec:method-hypotheses}, Appendix~\ref{app:hypotheses}), and two of its results
are only defensible because the rule predates the outcome: the conjunctive reader-sibling
test and the adaptation experiment's matched-baseline requirement
(Sections~\ref{sec:results-reader} and~\ref{sec:results-adaptation}).

\subsection{Reproducibility mechanics}

Three mechanical disciplines carry the reproducibility weight in this thesis, and they
are background because none is novel. \emph{Determinism}: greedy decoding makes every
reported figure a function of model, prompt and input (Section~\ref{sec:bg-local}).
\emph{Artefact retention}: every generation --- transcripts, structurer outputs, scores
--- is persisted, so a scoring change can be re-applied to frozen outputs and measured
in isolation from model changes, the mechanism Chapter~\ref{ch:measurement-validity}
depends on. \emph{Recorded provenance}: runs are tracked with their configuration,
split hashes and environment, and the data itself is documented in datasheet form
\parencite{gebru2018datasheets} (Appendix~\ref{app:heldout}). None of this guarantees
correctness; it guarantees that an error, once suspected, can be located --- which
Chapter~\ref{ch:measurement-validity} demonstrates repeatedly.
