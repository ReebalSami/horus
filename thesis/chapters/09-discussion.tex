\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

\section{Answers to the Research Questions}
\label{sec:disc-rqs}

The guiding question of Section~\ref{sec:research-questions} had two halves: how reliably local
open-weights document models convert German business-to-business invoices into a structured
representation on commodity hardware, and which stage of the pipeline limits that reliability.

\textbf{On the first half: reliably enough to be useful under review, and not reliably enough to
be unsupervised.} On real invoices, zero-shot and with open-weights models only, header-field
extraction reaches the high eighties on the headline aggregation
(Table~\ref{tab:heldout-headline}) --- reading on target-class hardware, structuring at the
floor, as that table's protocol note records. More important than the level is the shape: precision
substantially exceeds recall, so the dominant failure is an empty field, not an invented
one. That is the profile a reviewed workflow can absorb. A practitioner checking a booking sees
a gap and fills it; a practitioner cannot see a plausible fabrication. The system is therefore
a credible assistant to a reviewer and not a replacement for one, and nothing in these results
supports a stronger claim.

\textbf{On the second half, which is the thesis's central finding: reading limits the pipeline,
not structuring.} The same structuring model scores far higher on text rendered from the
reference than on the reading model's transcript, in every field cluster
(Table~\ref{tab:attribution-clusters}), and roughly half of all individual errors are values the
transcript never contained (Table~\ref{tab:attribution-shares}). Section~\ref{sec:disc-reading-constraint}
develops what follows.

The four sub-questions are answered as follows.

\textbf{(1) How do recent open-weights document models compare as the reading stage, holding
the structuring stage constant?}
They differ less than expected on aggregate score and more than expected in \emph{failure mode}
--- and the aggregate comparison turned out not to be decidable. On directly measured reading
quality the two finalists sat within a point of each other, and their ordering inverted when
the scoring corrections of Chapter~\ref{ch:measurement-validity} were applied to identical
transcripts; end-to-end score favoured one of them beforehand and was not re-taken for both
afterwards (Section~\ref{sec:results-reader}). The honest answer is therefore that the aggregate
instruments do not separate them, and that selecting on them would have been arbitrary. What does separate them is
qualitative: one model transcribes characters near-flawlessly but silently drops letterhead and
footer regions, which is exactly where a German invoice prints tax identifiers and bank details;
the other reads every region but occasionally slips a character inside a value
(Section~\ref{sec:results-reader}). For this application the second is preferable, because a corrupted
value is attached to a named field and can be checked against the page, whereas a dropped block
produces an absence indistinguishable from a field the document never carried.

\textbf{(2) What evidence supports splitting reading from structuring, and what remains
unproven?} On accuracy, the evidence gathered here favours the split --- but it is weaker than the
question deserves and is labelled accordingly. The comparison was made on a small
development subset before the scoring corrections, and it is reported as a preliminary study
(Section~\ref{sec:results-preliminary}). Its qualitative outcome was clear enough to determine the
architecture, and it is consistent with the published finding that constraining a model's output
format degrades its performance instead of being a free convenience \parencite{tam2024format}. The
attribution result then supplies an independent reason to prefer the split arrangement that has
nothing to do with accuracy: a two-stage pipeline lets the two failure sources be
\emph{measured separately}, and a single-shot model does not. Given that the measurement turned
out to be where most of this project's value lay, that is not a minor advantage.

\textbf{(3) What accuracy is attainable inside a commodity local envelope, and which stage pays
for it?} At the target class --- one modern 24\,\ac{GB} graphics processor --- the answer is
the headline itself: every reading figure in this thesis was produced on exactly that class of
machine, and nothing reported required more (Section~\ref{sec:method-repro}). The structuring stage
was additionally measured at the floor, on the 16\,\ac{GB} laptop, in the 4-bit precision it
is deployed in (Table~\ref{tab:sealed-val-arms}); full numeric precision is worth a small but
measurable amount, so quantisation costs something real and modest. What remains unmeasured is
the floor's \emph{reading} quality: the laptop's memory ceiling forces a pixel cap that reads
an A4 page at roughly half resolution, so while the pipeline loads and runs there, what it
scores there on real documents is a missing number, and Section~\ref{sec:lim-hardware} names it as a
limitation rather than assuming the gap away. What the floor demonstrably rules out is
\emph{training}: the adaptation experiments exceeded the laptop outright --- and then ran,
like everything else, within the same single 24\,\ac{GB} card. A company should size for the
target class, can expect the structuring stage to run even below it, and should expect the
reading stage to be the part that pays for any smaller machine.

\textbf{(4) Where does the residual error originate?} In three places, in this order of
magnitude: in reading, in the measurement, and only then in structuring. The measurement share
is the finding that was not anticipated. Several individual scoring corrections moved reported
figures further than any modelling decision in this project
(Chapter~\ref{ch:measurement-validity}), and the structuring stage --- once measured fairly and
given clean text --- is close to its ceiling
(Table~\ref{tab:sealed-val-arms}). The ordering matters because it inverts the intuitive
engineering priority, which is the subject of the next section.

\section{Reading as the Binding Constraint}
\label{sec:disc-reading-constraint}

The attribution result has a direct consequence for how effort should be spent, and it runs
against the grain of how such systems are usually improved.

\textbf{Prompt and schema engineering have a low ceiling while the transcript is poor.} If half
of all errors are values that never reached the structuring model, then no amount of work on the
field specification, the output schema or the instruction wording can recover them. The
structuring model cannot place a value it was never shown. This is not a claim that
specification work is worthless --- the audit of
Chapter~\ref{ch:measurement-validity} found real specification defects, and repairing them
mattered --- but it bounds what that work can achieve. The formal classification of every
underperforming field before the adaptation experiment
(Section~\ref{sec:results-adaptation}) found zero warranted specification repairs remaining, which is
that ceiling being reached in practice.

\textbf{The cheapest large gain is a better reader.} Between the survey reader and the selected
reader, end-to-end score improved substantially on identical everything else
(Table~\ref{tab:reader-lineage}), for the cost of changing one model identifier in a
configuration file. No structuring-side intervention attempted in this project produced a
comparable gain, and the one that was attempted seriously produced a regression.

\textbf{The finding generalises beyond this corpus, and one number outside this thesis says so
more convincingly than anything inside it.} A multilingual document-parsing benchmark published
during this work reports that open-source parsers lose an average of 17.8\,\% on photographed
versus digital documents, and that the collapse is concentrated in exactly the conditions this
thesis found hardest \parencite{mdpbench2026}. Their measurement is of reading quality, on 3{,}400
documents in 17 languages, with no structuring stage involved at all, and their degraded
condition --- printed pages photographed under controlled adverse conditions --- is harsher
than the deskewed scan-app captures here (Section~\ref{sec:method-data}). The directional agreement
between their result and the eleven-point channel gap measured here
(Table~\ref{tab:heldout-by-channel}) is the strongest external support this thesis has for its
central claim.

\textbf{What this implies for a practitioner is unglamorous.} The highest-leverage intervention
is not a better language model, a cleverer prompt or a fine-tune. It is improving the input:
scanning at higher resolution, capturing pages flat and evenly lit, preferring an
email-native \ac{PDF} over any camera capture of a printout whenever the choice exists. Those are
document-handling decisions, not machine-learning ones, and the measurements here suggest they
dominate.

\section{Why the Adaptation Failed}
\label{sec:disc-finetune}

The adaptation result is negative in all four cells, and a negative result is only useful if it
comes with a mechanism. It does, and the mechanism is one the literature predicts.

\textbf{The mechanism observed here.} The adapter did not lose the ability to place values. It
gained a propensity to emit them where the document carries none: the spurious-emission rate
rises markedly above its own baseline (Table~\ref{tab:finetune-grid}). The failure is a shift in
\emph{when the model answers}, not in \emph{how well it answers}.

That is precisely what supervised fine-tuning has been reported to do.
Uluoglakci and Taskaya Temizel \parencite{uluoglakci2026humility} state the premise directly: models hallucinate partly
because supervised fine-tuning ``implicitly rewards always responding''. Their study runs 800
controlled adaptation runs across two model families --- one of which is the immediate
predecessor generation of the structuring model used here --- and their remedy is to train
\emph{for} the admission of uncertainty rather than against it. This thesis's training data
contained no examples of appropriate abstention as a taught behaviour; every training target was
a filled record. On that reading, the adapter learned exactly what it was shown.

Three further results in that literature line up with what was measured.

\textbf{Adaptation of this kind learns style, not knowledge.} Ghosh et~al.\ \parencite{ghosh2024limitations}
report that low-rank adaptation ``is limited to learning response initiation and style
tokens''. If the intervention cannot add capability and can adjust output
disposition, then a task already near its ceiling on clean input
(Table~\ref{tab:sealed-val-arms}) has nothing to gain and a disposition to lose. The perfect-text
adapter evaluated on perfect text --- the cell with the cleanest input and the least excuse ---
regresses too, which is consistent with this and not with a simple overfitting story.

\textbf{Unfamiliar training examples govern how a model hallucinates.}
Kang et~al.\ \parencite{kang2024unfamiliar} find that the character of a fine-tuned model's hallucinations
is controlled by the unfamiliar examples in its training set. The transcript-trained adapter was fed exactly
that: noisy transcripts containing artefacts, dropped regions and character slips. Its
spurious-emission rate is the highest of any arm measured in this thesis.

\textbf{Freezing parameters preserves task performance while reducing hallucination.}
Kaplan et~al.\ \parencite{kaplan2026why} frame the effect as a stability--plasticity trade-off and report
that suppressing factual plasticity by freezing parameter groups can preserve task
performance while reducing hallucination. The intervention here did the opposite: it added plasticity
to the text tower of a model that was already competent at the task.

\textbf{What the negative result does and does not license.} It licenses the claim that
\emph{this} recipe --- one rank, one learning rate, one schedule, 100 examples, no sweep --- does
not improve this pipeline, and that the failure mode is over-emission rather than degradation.
It does not license the claim that adaptation cannot help this task. A larger and more diverse
training set, examples that teach abstention, a lower rank, or adaptation of the \emph{reading}
stage rather than the structuring stage are all untested and all plausible. The honest summary is
that the intervention was aimed at the stage that was not the bottleneck, and it is worth
noting that the attribution result had already said so before the experiment ran. The experiment
proceeded because the scope decision had made it conditional on a threshold that was met, not
because the attribution evidence recommended it.

\section{Measuring Before Optimising}
\label{sec:disc-measurement}

The methodological argument of this thesis is stated in Chapter~\ref{ch:measurement-validity};
this section says why it generalises.

\textbf{The bias is structural, not incidental.} Any evaluation that compares a
page-reading system against machine-readable reference data inherits a systematic gap between
\emph{as printed} and \emph{as stored}. The reference records a date as an ordered numeric
string; the page prints it day-first. The reference stores a controlled-vocabulary code; the page
prints a phrase. The reference omits an optional zero; the page implies one. Every one of these
punishes a correct reading, and the sum is not small. It follows from the shape of the
evaluation rather than from this corpus or this schema, so any study built the same way has
it.

\textbf{The bias is invisible in aggregates.} This is the part that makes it dangerous. Every
defect described in Chapter~\ref{ch:measurement-validity} was found by opening individual
documents; none was visible in a corpus-level number. The scores were plausible before
correction and plausible after. A reported figure carries no signal about whether the instrument
that produced it was sound, and the same is true of the retracted answer key and of the
precision confound: all three produced correctly computed, reproducible, plausible numbers that
meant something other than what they appeared to mean.

\textbf{Validating the instrument therefore belongs in the method, not in a footnote.} A study
that reports extraction scores without describing how the comparison handles representation
differences has not given the reader enough to judge the numbers, because the size of the
correction is comparable to the size of the effects being reported. The practices that carried
the weight here are collected in Section~\ref{sec:validity-generalises}; the cheapest and most
valuable is simply to archive generations, which makes a scoring change measurable in isolation.

\textbf{One consequence is uncomfortable and should be said plainly.} If a naive comparison
understates performance by an amount comparable to the differences between models, then
published comparisons that do not document their comparison rules are difficult to interpret,
and rankings drawn from them may not be robust. This thesis observed exactly that within its own
data: the ordering of two candidate readers on directly measured reading quality inverted when
the ruler was corrected (Section~\ref{sec:results-reader}).

\section{Local-Only Viability, Honestly Costed}
\label{sec:disc-local}

The premise of this work is that the document never leaves the company
(Section~\ref{sec:motivation}). It is worth stating what that premise costs and where it held.

\textbf{Inference fits both tiers; training fits only the target class; and the reading stage
pays for the smaller machine.} Both models
of the full pipeline load and run on a laptop with 16\,\ac{GB} of unified memory --- but only
with the reader's input capped to roughly half an A4 page's resolution, which is why every
reported transcript was produced on target-class hardware instead (Section~\ref{sec:method-repro}).
The adaptation experiments exceeded the laptop outright and ran on that same class: a single
24\,\ac{GB} graphics processor was enough to train, evaluate and re-baseline both adapters. A
company that owns such a workstation can therefore both run and adapt this system without any
document leaving the premises. A company that owns only the floor can run it --- at a reading
quality this thesis did not measure --- but would have to rent for adaptation, which means the
training documents leave the premises and reintroduces the problem the design exists to
avoid. Since the adaptation produced no benefit here, that trade did not have to be made; had
it produced one, the confidentiality argument would have had to be re-examined for the
floor-only company.

\textbf{Quantisation costs a small, measurable amount.} Table~\ref{tab:sealed-val-arms} gives
the difference between 4-bit and full precision on the same corpus. The deployable configuration
is the weaker one, and that gap is part of the price of the local constraint.

\textbf{The measurement itself broke the premise, deliberately and on record.} Two of the
three channels used to build the held-out answer key are cloud services, so the content of
those 39 invoices left local hardware (Section~\ref{sec:method-heldout-gt}); and the
native-resolution pass that transcribed them ran on the rented target-class instance
(Section~\ref{sec:method-repro}); that pass ran open weights under the author's control rather
than a third-party model service, a different privacy class, but off-premises nonetheless. The distinction
relied on --- that a
measuring instrument is not part of the delivered system --- is sound as far as it goes: nothing
on the inference path makes a network call. But it is worth being clear that a practitioner
bound by professional secrecy could not have built this answer key, or reproduced this
measurement, on their own client documents within the constraint. The evaluation methodology
is therefore not itself deployable inside the constraint it
evaluates, and closing that gap --- an adjudication process that runs entirely locally --- is
listed as future work.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:disc-threats}

Limitations proper are collected in Chapter~\ref{ch:limitations-future-work}. This section
concerns threats to the validity of the inferences drawn above.

\subsection{Internal validity}

\textbf{Leakage.} The validation split was sealed with a recorded hash before any adaptation
work; prompt design used only the fitting set; checkpoint selection used a slice carved from the
fitting set, not the validation set (Section~\ref{sec:method-splits}). The residual risk is the one
that leaves no trace: the author read validation documents during the defect investigations of
Chapter~\ref{ch:measurement-validity}. Those investigations changed \emph{scoring rules}, never
prompts, and every rule change is symmetric and test-guarded --- but a scoring rule authored
after seeing which cells failed is not a fully blind instrument, and no claim here should be read
as if it were.

\textbf{Determinism.} All generations use greedy decoding, so no reported figure depends on a
sampling seed. The cost is that no variance estimate over decoding randomness is available:
single-run figures cannot carry confidence intervals of that kind, and differences of a few
thousandths should not be read as meaningful. Where a paired comparison mattered --- the two
reader finalists --- document-level variation was tested directly instead, with an exact
McNemar test over the paired per-cell outcomes (Section~\ref{sec:results-reader}), which requires
no repeated sampling.

\textbf{Precision matching.} Every adaptation comparison is made against a baseline measured in
the same numeric precision (Table~\ref{tab:precision-confound}). This threat was identified in
advance, not discovered afterwards, which is the only reason it was controlled.

\subsection{Construct validity}

\textbf{Does the metric measure legal usability?} No, and this should not be overstated. F$_1$
over registered header fields measures extraction correctness. It does not measure whether a
booking would be accepted by a tax authority, whether the invoice satisfies the mandatory
particulars of \S14~\ac{UStG}, or whether an input-tax deduction would survive audit. The
weighted, compliance-aware metric that would have gestured at this was abandoned for the reason
given in Section~\ref{sec:method-metrics}. What is reported is a technical measurement with a legal
motivation, not a legal assessment.

\textbf{Is the reading proxy faithful?} Partially, with a known blind spot. It asks whether a
reference value appears in the transcript at all, which a transcript containing every value in
scrambled order would satisfy while being useless downstream
(Section~\ref{sec:method-answerability}). The proxy was therefore never used alone, and the one
decision where it mattered was settled by hand-judging every individual miss instead of
trusting the aggregate.

\textbf{Is the perfect-text ceiling a fair upper bound?} Only after its own defects were
repaired. Five were found (Section~\ref{sec:validity-apparatus}), two of which made fields score
nothing because the renderer labelled them with schema vocabulary no invoice prints --- the
values were there, under a wording the model could not map to the field. A renderer defect makes
the ceiling wrong in an unpredictable direction, not merely optimistic, so a ceiling reported
without validating the renderer would not have been interpretable.

\subsection{External validity}

\textbf{Corpus scale.} The real-document result rests on 39 invoices. That supports a claim about
direction and rough magnitude; it does not support a precise figure, and the per-channel cells
contain as few as ten documents each. The independent corroboration of the channel effect
\parencite{mdpbench2026} is what makes that particular finding safer than its sample size alone would
justify.

\textbf{Single annotator.} The held-out answer key was adjudicated by one person --- the author
--- so no inter-annotator agreement can be reported. The three-channel design and the
automatic-acceptance rule reduce the exposure, in that 463 of the decided cells rest on either
printed-text proof or two independent channels agreeing, not on judgement
(Section~\ref{sec:method-heldout-gt}). The remaining 248 do rest on judgement, and that is an
irreducible limitation of a single-author corpus.

\textbf{One document class, two languages, one country.} Everything here concerns
business-to-business invoices under German and European conventions. Nothing is claimed about
receipts, contracts, bank statements or correspondence, and nothing about jurisdictions with
different invoicing rules.

\textbf{Synthetic pages dominate the corpus.} The larger corpus is born-digital, so its pages
are clean by construction (Section~\ref{sec:method-data}). That is exactly why the held-out corpus
exists, and the eleven-point gap between clean and phone-scanned documents
(Table~\ref{tab:heldout-by-channel}) is the size of the error a reader would make by
generalising from the synthetic corpus alone.

\textbf{A moving target.} The models evaluated here were released within roughly a year of this
work. The field moves quickly enough that specific model rankings will not survive long. The
findings that should survive are the ones about method: that reading is the binding constraint at
this scale, that the measuring apparatus must be validated before it is trusted, and that
degraded capture costs more than language does.
