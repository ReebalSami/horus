\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}

German tax advisors, auditors and lawyers process large volumes of client
documents --- incoming invoices and supporting receipts --- as a daily core
activity. Recent progress in document \acp{VLM} makes it tempting to
automate this work with cloud services. For these professions, however, the
question is not only one of data protection: it also touches professional
secrecy, which is protected by criminal law.

Two bodies of law bear on that temptation, and they are separate.
\emph{Data protection} governs the processing of personal data, and a
cloud arrangement addresses it through a processor contract, appropriate
technical measures and processing inside the \ac{EU} (Art.~28, 32 \ac{DSGVO}
\parencite{dsgvo28,dsgvo32}). It does not, however, dispose of the question:
the \ac{EDPB} holds that a processor established inside Europe may still be
reached by third-country law, that contract wording to that effect does not
release it from its obligations, and that where the recipient falls under
United States surveillance law, contractual and organisational measures alone
will generally not restore an equivalent level of protection --- only technical
ones can \parencite{edpb2024processors,edpb2021supplementary}.

\emph{Professional secrecy} is the second body, it is backed by criminal law,
and it is the one that binds harder here for two reasons that are easy to miss.
The first is subject matter: it protects everything entrusted in the exercise of
the profession, including the affairs of \emph{legal persons}, where data
protection is concerned with natural ones \parencite{bstbk2026}. A thesis about
business-to-business invoices therefore sits almost entirely inside the stricter
regime and only partly inside the better-known one. The second is that
disclosure to an outside provider is permitted only on conditions the provider
must actively meet: the provider has to be selected with care and bound to
confidentiality in text form, instructed about the criminal consequences of a
breach, and restricted to the knowledge the service actually requires;
services performed abroad additionally require a level of secrecy protection
comparable to the domestic one; and where the service serves one specific client
matter rather than the company's general infrastructure, the client's own consent is
required as well.\footnote{\S203~\ac{StGB} \parencite{stgb203} names these
professions --- \textit{Steuerberater}, \textit{Wirtschaftspr\"ufer},
\textit{Rechtsanw\"alte} --- as holders of protected secrets and, since the 2017
reform, both permits disclosure to contributing outside persons where necessary
and makes the professional criminally liable for failing to bind them. The
contractual
requirements are set out separately for each profession, in near-identical terms:
\S62a~\ac{StBerG} for tax advisors \parencite{stberg62a}, \S43e~\ac{BRAO} for
lawyers \parencite{brao43e}, and \S50a~\ac{WPO} for auditors
\parencite{wpo50a}, each attaching to that profession's own confidentiality duty
--- \S57 Abs.~1~\ac{StBerG} \parencite{stberg57}, \S43a Abs.~2~\ac{BRAO} and
\S43~\ac{WPO} respectively.} Both chambers make the practical consequence explicit,
and it is the point most often elided: a data-protection processing agreement is
\emph{not} the same instrument and does not satisfy the confidentiality
requirement, and the two obligations stand side by side rather than one absorbing
the other \parencite{bstbk2026,brak2024ki,bstbkdsgvo}.

These conditions are not categorically unmeetable. One major cloud provider
publishes an amendment written specifically for holders of professional secrets
and referring to the criminal provision by name.\footnote{Microsoft's
\emph{Zusatzvereinbarung f\"ur Berufsgeheimnistr\"ager} / German Data Secrecy
Amendment \parencite{msprofsecrecy}. Two limits are worth noting: the amendment
runs for 36 months from acceptance with no renewal clause while the framework
agreement it supplements is open-ended, and published university assessments of
it record gaps specifically for \ac{AI} services. Of the remaining large
providers, one supplies a comparable document only on request under a
non-disclosure agreement and one has no published equivalent at all.} What
does not currently exist is such an amendment from the providers offering the
strongest general-purpose language models directly: their published terms
address data protection and are silent on professional
secrecy \parencite{openaidpa,anthropicdpa}, and the route that does carry the
amendment reaches those models only through a cloud platform
\parencite{azureopenaieu}. Independently of any contract, providers under United
States jurisdiction remain subject to extraterritorial access powers --- which is
precisely the residual risk the comparable-secrecy test exists to address.

Local inference removes the question rather than managing it: the document never
leaves the company, so no provider has to be selected, bound, instructed, monitored
or consented to. This
thesis takes that constraint as its operational premise --- not as its research
contribution --- and asks what is technically achievable \emph{within} it.

\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem}

Working locally confines the system to models that fit on commodity, on-premises
hardware. This raises a concrete empirical question: how reliably do small,
locally hosted, open-weights document \acp{VLM} --- the wave of specialised models
released from late~2025 onward --- convert German B2B invoices into a structured,
legally meaningful form? The closest prior work benchmarked cloud-frontier models
and general-purpose open-weights models on invoice extraction, but included none
of the recent \emph{specialised} open-weights document \acp{VLM} and no German
B2B corpus \parencite{berghaus2025}. German invoice extraction itself is not
unstudied --- it has been addressed with a recognition-plus-layout model on a
restricted real German corpus \parencite{thiee2023invoices}, adapting an approach the
same group had developed on English invoices \parencite{krieger2021german} --- but not
with this model class, not against a machine-exact reference under the European semantic standard,
and not under a hardware constraint. That intersection is the gap this thesis
addresses; \S\ref{sec:rw-german} makes the comparison with the German literature
explicit and \S\ref{sec:rw-positioning} returns to where the closest benchmark's
findings and this thesis's diverge.

A second problem emerges only once such a system is measured seriously. An
end-to-end extraction score conflates three very different failure sources: the
model may fail to \emph{read} the page, it may read correctly but fail to
\emph{structure} what it read, or the \emph{measurement itself} may be unfair ---
penalising an answer that is in fact correct as printed. Separating these is a
prerequisite for spending effort in the right place, and it turns out to change the
engineering conclusion substantially.

\section{Research Questions}
\label{sec:research-questions}

The guiding question of this thesis is:

\begin{quote}
\itshape
How reliably can current open-weights, locally hosted document \acp{VLM} convert
German business-to-business invoices into a legally meaningful structured
representation on commodity on-premises hardware --- and which stage of that
pipeline limits the achievable reliability?
\end{quote}

\noindent It is addressed through four sub-questions. They are worded to match
what this thesis actually measures, which is narrower than the general form each
question could take; \S\ref{sec:rq-map} states for each one where it is answered
and how strong that answer is.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item\label{rq:readers} How do recent open-weights document \acp{VLM} compare
        with one another \emph{as the reading stage} of a German e-invoice
        extraction pipeline, holding the structuring stage constant?
  \item\label{rq:split} What evidence supports splitting the task into a reading
        stage and a structuring stage rather than using a single model that goes
        from page image straight to structured output --- and what about that
        comparison remains unproven?
  \item\label{rq:envelope} What accuracy is attainable inside the memory envelope
        of commodity local hardware, and which stage pays for that envelope?
  \item\label{rq:attribution} Where does the residual error actually originate
        --- in reading, in structuring, or in the measurement?
\end{enumerate}

\noindent These are made falsifiable through a hypothesis set registered before
the corresponding data was collected; Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology} states the
hypotheses and the pre-registration discipline. Part of that set concerns system
layers that were subsequently placed outside the scope of this thesis; those
hypotheses are reported as registered-but-not-evaluated in
Chapter~\ref{ch:limitations-future-work} rather than quietly dropped.

\subsection*{What each question is answered with}
\label{sec:rq-map}

A question can be answered by a sealed measurement, by a diagnostic instrument,
by a preliminary study, or not at all, and those are not interchangeable. The
mapping is stated here so that no answer later in the thesis has to be weighed
against a promise made in this chapter.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Question~\ref{rq:readers}} --- answered by a sealed measurement of
        reading quality plus a hand-judged audit of every residual miss
        (\S\ref{sec:results-reader}). The finding is partly negative: the
        aggregate instruments did not separate the two finalists, so the decision
        rests on failure mode rather than on score. One finalist's end-to-end
        score was never re-taken under the corrected scoring rules, and that
        asymmetry is stated where the table appears.
  \item \textbf{Question~\ref{rq:split}} --- answered \emph{preliminarily} on
        accuracy (\S\ref{sec:results-preliminary}: in-sample, small, predating
        the scoring corrections) and \emph{decisively} on measurability
        (\S\ref{sec:design-two-stage}). Whether a single-shot arrangement
        overtakes the split within this model class is left open on purpose
        (\S\ref{sec:rw-berghaus}).
  \item \textbf{Question~\ref{rq:envelope}} --- answered at two tiers carrying
        different evidence. Nothing reported needed more than a workstation-class
        machine with a single 24\,\ac{GB} graphics processor, where every reading
        figure was produced; the structuring stage is additionally measured at the
        floor of a 16\,\ac{GB} laptop, in its deployed precision
        (Table~\ref{tab:sealed-val-arms}). The one missing number is the floor's
        reading quality --- the laptop caps the reader at half resolution --- which
        \S\ref{sec:method-repro} records and \S\ref{sec:lim-hardware} carries as a
        limitation. Throughput is deliberately not claimed: it was instrumented
        earlier in the project and the confirmatory comparison was not run, so it
        is reported as registered-but-unevaluated (Appendix~\ref{app:hypotheses}).
  \item \textbf{Question~\ref{rq:attribution}} --- answered by three instruments
        that agree (\S\ref{sec:results-attribution}), and it is the question this
        thesis answers most firmly.
\end{itemize}

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

This thesis contributes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item an evaluation of recent \emph{specialised} open-weights local document
        \acp{VLM} on German e-invoice extraction --- the model class and corpus
        class absent from the closest prior benchmark \parencite{berghaus2025} ---
        together with a measurement on 39 \emph{real} invoices whose answer key
        was adjudicated across three independent reading channels;
  \item a \emph{measurement-validity} methodology, and the empirical finding that
        motivates it: a documented chain of reference-data and scoring defects,
        several of which moved reported scores further than any modelling change
        in this project, and one of which would have inverted a conclusion had it
        gone unnoticed (Chapter~\ref{ch:measurement-validity});
  \item an \emph{attribution} method that separates reading error from structuring
        error by replacing the transcript with text rendered from the reference,
        and by asking of every individual miss whether the value was present in
        the transcript at all --- yielding the thesis's central claim, that
        reading rather than structuring is the binding constraint at this scale;
  \item a reproducible negative result: parameter-efficient adaptation of the
        structuring stage fails in all four cells of a two-by-two design, and
        fails by a specific mechanism --- it raises the rate at which the model
        emits values for fields the document does not carry; and
  \item a locally deployable prototype --- HORUS, for Hybrid \ac{OCR}-free
        Reading and Understanding System --- and a reproducible evaluation
        harness as engineering artefacts.
\end{itemize}

\noindent One contribution claimed in an earlier plan for this work is
\emph{not} delivered, and is named here rather than quietly dropped. A
compliance-aware metric weighting fields by their legal importance under
\S14~\ac{UStG} and \S33~\ac{UStDV} was designed and then abandoned, because the
weights would have been chosen by the author and a headline number resting on an
author-chosen weight vector is comparable to nothing.
Section~\ref{sec:method-metrics} gives the reasoning; unweighted figures are
reported instead, with per-field results given in full so that a reader who wants
to weight them can.

\section{Scope and Delimitations}
\label{sec:scope}

This thesis is \emph{not} a startup pitch, a pure \ac{OCR} benchmark, or a
legal-tech treatise: professional secrecy frames the motivation but is not the
contribution. It does not claim to beat commercial systems in general; it
characterises a German-specific cell of the problem space where the local-only
constraint binds.

The evaluated scope is the document-reading and structuring layer. A knowledge
graph over the extracted data and natural-language querying over that graph were
designed (Chapter~\ref{ch:system-design}) but deliberately not built or evaluated;
so was a direct comparison against cloud models. Chapter~\ref{ch:limitations-future-work}
states what was left out and why.

\paragraph{A note on terminology.} \emph{\ac{OCR}-free} in this thesis is an
architectural statement, not a claim about model names: the pipeline contains no
separate character-recognition engine, and the vision-language model maps page
pixels directly to output tokens. Several models in the evaluated cohort ---
olmOCR-2 among them --- nevertheless carry \ac{OCR} in their names, because
their authors named them after the task they perform rather than after the
architecture they use. The two
usages are independent, and the naming is not a contradiction.

Chapter~\ref{ch:background} introduces the technical and legal background, and
Chapter~\ref{ch:related-work} positions the thesis against prior work.
Chapter~\ref{ch:system-design} presents the system design and states which parts
of it are evaluated here. Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology} specifies the corpora, the
ground truth, the metric suite and the pre-registration discipline.

Chapter~\ref{ch:measurement-validity} then does something a thesis does not
usually need a chapter for: it establishes that the measuring apparatus is sound
before any model claim is made with it. It is placed between the method and the
results because that is the order in which it has to be read --- the corrections
it describes are the reason the numbers in Chapter~\ref{ch:results} can be
trusted, and several of them are larger than anything a modelling decision
achieved.

Chapter~\ref{ch:results} reports the results, and
Chapter~\ref{ch:implementation} describes the prototype.
Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion} interprets the findings,
Chapter~\ref{ch:limitations-future-work} sets out the limitations and the work
left undone, and Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion} concludes.
