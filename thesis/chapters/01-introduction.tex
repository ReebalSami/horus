\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}

German tax advisors (\textit{Steuerberater}), auditors
(\textit{Wirtschaftspr\"ufer}), and lawyers (\textit{Rechtsanw\"alte}) process
large volumes of client documents --- incoming invoices
(\textit{Eingangsrechnungen}) and supporting receipts (\textit{Belege}) --- as a
daily core activity. Recent progress in document \ac{VLM}s makes it tempting to
automate this work with cloud services. For these professions, however, that
path is not merely a data-protection question but a criminal-law one.

Whereas the \ac{DSGVO} can be satisfied for cloud processing through a data
processing agreement and EU data residency, the professional-secrecy regime of
\S203~\ac{StGB} in conjunction with \S62a~\ac{StBerG} imposes a stricter,
separate requirement: a confidentiality obligation in text form for any external
service provider that gains access to client data \cite{bstbk2026}. As of early
2026 the major cloud \ac{LLM} providers do not offer such an agreement, and
foreign providers additionally fail the comparable-secrecy test of
\S62a~Abs.~4~\ac{StBerG}. The practical consequence is sharp: for this specific
professional context, \emph{local} inference is currently the only legally
watertight option. This thesis takes that constraint as its operational premise
--- not as its research contribution --- and asks what is technically achievable
\emph{within} it.

\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem}

The constraint rules out the strongest cloud models and confines the system to
models that run on commodity, on-premise hardware. This raises a concrete
empirical question: how far do small, locally hosted, open-source document
\ac{VLM}s --- the wave of specialised models released from late~2025 onward ---
close the gap to cloud state of the art on the German B2B accounting workflow,
and where does reasoning over an automatically constructed \ac{KG} add value
over flat vector retrieval? Prior work on invoice processing with vision models
\cite{berghaus2025} benchmarked large cloud models but did not include the
recent specialised open-source document \ac{VLM}s; that omission is the gap this
thesis addresses.

\section{Research Questions}
\label{sec:research-questions}

The guiding question of this thesis is:

\begin{quote}
\itshape
How well can current open-source local document \acp{VLM} handle the German B2B
accounting workflow end to end (extraction $\rightarrow$ knowledge graph
$\rightarrow$ analytical querying), and where does compliance-aware reasoning
over an automatically constructed knowledge graph beat flat vector retrieval?
\end{quote}

\noindent It is decomposed along the three system layers and made falsifiable
through a pre-registered hypothesis set (H1--H6, H8;
see Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}).

  \item an image-to-\ac{KG} fidelity study, extending the text-to-\ac{KG}

  \item a characterisation of \emph{when} graph retrieval beats flat vector
        retrieval on German accounting corpora, relating to \cite{han2025}; and
  \item a privacy-preserving, locally deployable prototype as an engineering
        artefact.
\end{itemize}

\section{Scope and Delimitations}
\label{sec:scope}

This thesis is \emph{not} a startup pitch, a pure \ac{OCR} benchmark, or a
legal-tech treatise: \S203~\ac{StGB} frames the motivation but is not the
contribution. It does not claim to beat commercial systems in general; it
characterises a German-specific cell of the problem space where the local-only
constraint binds.
