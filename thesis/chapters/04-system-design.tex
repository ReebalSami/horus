\chapter{System Design}
\label{ch:system-design}

This chapter describes the system that was designed, and states precisely which part of it
was built and measured. The distinction matters enough to be made in three places --- in
the architecture figure, in the section headings, and in a closing section that states the
scope in plain words --- because a design chapter is the easiest place in a thesis to let an
intention read as an achievement.

The system was conceived as three layers. One of them was implemented and evaluated; the
other two remain design. \S\ref{sec:design-scope} explains why the scope was narrowed, and
Chapter~\ref{ch:limitations-future-work} lists the hypotheses that consequently went
untested instead of dropping them from the record.

\section{Design Overview}
\label{sec:design-overview}

The practitioner-facing problem stated in Chapter~\ref{ch:introduction} is not
\emph{transcribe this invoice}. It is closer to \emph{which of my clients' suppliers billed
reverse-charge in the last quarter, and can I prove each of those bookings from the
document}. Answering that requires three capabilities that are naturally separable, and the
design separates them (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \input{figures/architecture.tex}
  \caption[The three-layer design]{The three-layer design. Layer~1 was built and measured;
  Layers~2 and~3 are design only and are marked as such wherever they appear.}
  \label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Layer~1 --- reading and structuring.} A page image becomes a validated record: the
values a human would read off the page, placed into named fields with their types
canonicalised. This layer is the subject of the rest of the thesis.

\textbf{Layer~2 --- entity resolution and linking.} Validated records become nodes and edges
in a graph of parties, documents and obligations: the same supplier recognised across
invoices and periods, each document linked to its issuer, its recipient and its tax
treatment.

\textbf{Layer~3 --- querying and reporting.} Practitioner questions answered over that
graph, with every answer traceable back to the document and field it came from.

\subsection{The honest-null contract}
\label{sec:design-honest-null}

One commitment binds every layer and is stated here because the rest of the design follows
from it. \emph{A field the document does not carry must come back absent, and a field the
system could not read must come back absent --- never guessed.}

The justification is specific to the domain, not a general preference for caution. The
output of this system is not consumed by a person reading a summary; it is consumed by a
professional who signs off a booking and carries liability for it. An empty field is visible to
that person: it prompts a look at the document. A plausible fabrication --- a well-formed tax
identifier that belongs to no one, a due date that was never printed --- is not visible,
because it looks exactly like a correct answer. The failure mode that matters here is
therefore not a low score; it is a confident wrong answer that survives review.

Three design consequences follow, and each is visible later in this chapter: the repair stage
may never invent a value (\S\ref{sec:design-validation}); the evaluation treats a correct
absence as a first-class outcome and measures the rate of values emitted for fields that do
not exist (\S\ref{sec:method-metrics}); and, in the adaptation study of
\S\ref{sec:results-adaptation}, a change that raises raw extraction while raising that rate is
read as a regression rather than an improvement.

\subsection{Why the reading layer is the load-bearing one}

The layers are not equal partners, and the asymmetry is worth stating before anything else,
because it is the reason the thesis ended up where it did.

Every layer above the first consumes the first layer's output. A supplier node is only as
correct as the seller name and tax identifier that produced it; a reverse-charge query is
only as correct as the tax category extracted from each document. Errors in the reading
layer do not stay in the reading layer --- they propagate upward and, in the graph, compound:
a misread tax identifier does not merely corrupt one field, it can split one supplier into
two entities and thereby corrupt every aggregate that supplier appears in.

The practical consequence is an ordering constraint on the work. The quality of the extraction
layer upper-bounds the quality of everything built on it, so a graph layer built on an
extraction layer of unknown reliability would produce results whose errors could not be
attributed: a wrong answer to a practitioner question would be indistinguishable from a
correctly answered question over wrong data. Establishing what the first layer actually
achieves is therefore a precondition for the other two being meaningful, not merely the first
item on a list.

That precondition turned out to be more demanding than anticipated, which is what
\S\ref{sec:design-scope} is about.

\section{Layer 1: Reading and Structuring}
\label{sec:design-layer1}

This is the layer implemented and evaluated in this thesis. Its input is a page image; its
output is a record whose fields are named by an externally defined schema and whose values
are canonicalised strings. Everything between those two points is shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \input{figures/pipeline.tex}
  \caption[The Layer~1 pipeline]{The Layer~1 pipeline: three stages, the artefact that
  passes between them, and the point at which the two transcript-level measurement
  instruments attach. The transcript boundary is what makes the reading and structuring
  stages separately accountable; \S\ref{sec:method-attribution} defines the instruments
  and \S\ref{sec:results-attribution} reports what they found.}
  \label{fig:pipeline}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Two stages, not one}
\label{sec:design-two-stage}

The obvious arrangement is one model: hand a vision-language model the page image and a
schema, and ask it for the filled record directly. The design here does not do that. It uses
two models in sequence --- a \textbf{reader} that transcribes the page to plain text with no
knowledge of the schema, and a \textbf{structurer} that receives that text plus the field
specification and emits the record.

Three arguments motivate the split; the first is architectural, the second empirical, and the
third is the one that made this thesis possible at all.

\textbf{Separation of concerns, and therefore of failure.} Reading a page and populating a
schema are different tasks with different failure modes. Reading fails on rendering: faint
print, an unusual layout, a photograph taken at an angle. Structuring fails on semantics: the
net total placed in the gross field, a label misinterpreted, a value that belongs to no field
emitted anyway. A single model that does both can fail either way and the output looks
identical --- a wrong value in a field. Two stages give each failure mode its own address.

\textbf{The two-stage arrangement measured better on this corpus.} This is not a purely
a-priori design choice. A probe compared instructing a reading model to emit structured data
directly against the two-stage arrangement, and a further study compared a rule-based
extraction baseline, a single-shot arrangement and the two-stage arrangement on a development
subset. The two-stage arrangement won in both; \S\ref{sec:results-preliminary} reports those
studies with the caveats they require. The finding also has support in the literature:
imposing a rigid output format on a generative model has been shown to degrade its
performance on the reasoning-adjacent part of the task instead of being a free convenience
\parencite{tam2024format}, and asking a reading model to simultaneously read and conform to a
schema is exactly that imposition.

\textbf{The transcript is a measurement surface.} This is the decisive argument, and it is
easy to overlook because it is about the researcher, not the user. A boundary between
the stages is a place where an intermediate artefact exists, can be stored, and can be
manipulated. Because the transcript exists, the reading stage can be removed from the
measurement --- by substituting text rendered from the reference data --- and the structuring
stage can be measured against a ceiling that is independent of reading quality. Because the
transcript exists, each individual miss can be classified by whether the value was present in
it. A single-shot model offers no such surface: its intermediate representation is a tensor,
not an artefact, and the only observable is the final record. The central empirical result of
this thesis --- that reading rather than structuring is the binding constraint --- is a result
the single-shot architecture could not have produced.

\textbf{What the split costs.} The transcript is plain text, so
everything the page expressed through position survives the boundary only insofar as the
reader preserved it in reading order. A two-column layout, a table whose columns carry
meaning, a value printed in a box beside its label instead of after it --- all of these
survive only as well as the reader serialises them. The structuring stage cannot recover what
the transcript does not carry, and this is not a hypothetical cost: it is the mechanism behind
the attribution result of \S\ref{sec:results-attribution}. A single-shot model, whatever else
it gives up, never loses layout information at an internal boundary, because it has no such
boundary. The design accepts this cost in exchange for accountability, and the thesis reports
the size of the cost instead of assuming it away.

Two further consequences of the split are worth naming. The two stages run sequentially, so
latency is additive and two sets of model weights must fit within the same memory envelope ---
a real constraint on the hardware this system targets (\S\ref{sec:method-repro}). And each
stage can be replaced independently: changing the reading model is a configuration change that
requires nothing of the structuring stage, which is what made the reader comparison of
\S\ref{sec:results-reader} affordable.

\subsection{The target schema}
\label{sec:design-schema}

The extraction target is not invented for the experiment. It is an alignment to
\ac{EN}~16931 \parencite{en16931}, the European standard that defines the semantic model of an
electronic invoice and gives each element a business-term identifier. In Germany that standard
is the interoperability reference for business-to-business invoicing, so a system that extracts
to it produces records that mean the same thing to another system, and results that can be
compared with other work on the same terms.

The schema covers 34 flat header fields and three repeating groups. The flat fields fall into
recognisable blocks: document identity (number, issue date, document type, currency, delivery
date, order reference, billing period, document-level tax rate), the seller block (name,
address, \ac{VAT} identifier, tax number, location identifier), the buyer block (name, address,
\ac{VAT} identifier, reference), the payment block (due date, means, bank details, payment
reference) and the totals
block (line total, taxable base, tax total, grand total, amount due, prepaid amount,
allowances, charges, rounding). The repeating groups are the line-item table, the tax breakdown
per rate, and early-payment discount terms.

Three properties of the schema are deliberate design decisions.

\textbf{One registry drives three consumers.} The field set is declared once, in a single
registry, which generates the specification given to the structuring model, drives the scorer,
and drives the reporting. Had the specification and the scorer carried separate field lists,
they could have drifted --- a field asked for and never scored, or scored and never asked for
--- and the drift would have been invisible in the results.
\S\ref{sec:method-schema} treats the methodological consequences; the point here is that the
single-registry arrangement is an architectural property of the system, not a convention
upheld by discipline.

\textbf{Keys are canonical, values are as printed.} Field names are canonical English
identifiers derived from the standard; values are whatever the document prints, in whatever
language it prints them. No German label matching happens on the extraction path. This is what
makes the same pipeline applicable to the German and English documents of the held-out corpus
without a per-language variant, and therefore what makes a multilingual evaluation of one
system meaningful instead of an evaluation of two.

\textbf{The record is typed.} Each field declares a type --- money, date, rate, code, free text
--- and the type determines how a raw model value is canonicalised and how a predicted value is
compared with a reference one. A money field is not compared as a string; a date is not
compared as a string. Declaring the type once, at the registry, is what allows the comparison
rule to be applied identically to both sides of every comparison, a property
Chapter~\ref{ch:measurement-validity} shows to be easy to violate and expensive when violated.

\subsection{Validation and repair}
\label{sec:design-validation}

The third stage runs no model. It takes whatever text the structuring stage produced and turns
it into a record of the declared shape, or reports that it could not.

A stage of this kind is necessary because the local inference runtime used here offers no
constrained decoding: the structuring model cannot be forced to emit well-formed output at
generation time, so conformance has to be established afterwards. In practice models emit
records wrapped in explanatory prose, enclosed in code fences, followed by a chat-template
token, with trailing commas, with keys capitalised differently from the specification, or
truncated mid-structure at the generation limit. Discarding all of these as unparseable would
measure the model's formatting conventions, not its extraction.

The stage therefore applies a recovery sequence, in order, stopping at the first success:
parse the text directly; scan for the first balanced object, which handles both prose around
the record and a model that emitted the same object repeatedly; take the span between the
outermost braces; tolerate trailing commas; and finally supply structural delimiters the model
omitted. Every recovered object then has its keys matched case-insensitively against the
registry, and each value canonicalised by its declared type --- German and Anglo-Saxon decimal
conventions to one canonical form, the several date conventions a European invoice prints to
one canonical form, a rate with or without its percent sign to one canonical form.

One design principle governs the whole stage, and it is a hard constraint, not a
preference: \emph{repair may never invent a value}. The structural repair supplies delimiters
only, never a key and never a value, and it refuses to guess when the output was truncated in
the middle of a value --- in that case the field is reported absent. A value that cannot be
parsed becomes an absence, not a best guess. An unrecognised key is dropped, never
mapped to the field it most resembles. The reason is the honest-null contract of
\S\ref{sec:design-honest-null}: in this domain a plausible fabrication is worse than a gap,
because the gap is visible to the reviewer who has to sign the booking and the fabrication is
not. A repair stage that guesses is a fabrication engine with a reassuring name.

Two small behaviours follow the same principle. Where a document is multi-page, the record is
assembled page by page and the first non-absent value for a field wins, so a later page cannot
overwrite an earlier honest reading with a spurious one. And exactly one cross-field repair is
performed: where a document has a single tax rate in its breakdown and the flat tax-rate field
is absent, the value is copied across. That is a derivation from data the model did emit, not
an invention.

\textbf{What this stage deliberately does not do.} It does not check the arithmetic identities
that the invoice standard guarantees --- that line amounts sum to the line total, that base plus
tax equals the grand total, that the amount due follows from the grand total and any prepayment.
Those checks would catch a class of reading error that is otherwise silent, and they are a
natural extension of the stage; they are not implemented here, and \S\ref{sec:fw-production}
records that as future work instead of describing them as if they existed. Nothing in this
thesis's results depends on a consistency check that was not performed.

\subsection{Designed to be auditable}
\label{sec:design-audit}

One further property is a design decision, not an implementation detail, because it
shaped what could later be claimed.

Every intermediate artefact is persisted: the rasterised page, the transcript, the raw text the
structuring model emitted, the parsed record, and the per-field comparison outcome. Nothing in
the pipeline is transient. Combined with greedy decoding --- no sampling anywhere, so a run is
reproducible from its configuration --- this means a stored result can be re-examined and
re-scored without re-running any model.

That capability is the reason Chapter~\ref{ch:measurement-validity} exists in the form it does.
When a defect was found in the scoring rules, the affected results could be recomputed on the
identical stored model outputs, which isolates the effect of the correction from any change in
model behaviour. Had the artefacts not been kept, every correction would have required a fresh
inference run, and the difference between \emph{the ruler changed} and \emph{the model changed}
would have been unrecoverable.

\section{Layer 2 (Design Only): Compliance-Aware Knowledge Graph}
\label{sec:design-layer2}

\emph{Not implemented or evaluated within the scope of this thesis. Everything in this section
describes an intended design; no claim in this thesis rests on it. See
\S\ref{sec:design-scope} and Chapter~\ref{ch:limitations-future-work}.}

\subsection{Why a graph rather than a document store}

The questions a tax practitioner actually asks are relational. \emph{Which suppliers billed us
under reverse charge last quarter, and is each of those treatments consistent with that
supplier's other invoices?} \emph{Has this invoice already been booked under a different
number?} \emph{Which documents in this period are missing a particular that the law requires?}

Each of these is a question about relationships between documents, not about the content of one
document. A store that treats each invoice as an independent record answers them only by
scanning and re-deriving the relationships at query time; a representation that makes the
relationships explicit answers them directly. That is the argument for a graph, and it is an
argument about the shape of the question, not about technology fashion.

\subsection{The intended model}

Validated records from Layer~1 would become nodes: legal parties, documents, line items, tax
rates and periods, with edges for issuance, receipt, reference and tax treatment. Entity
resolution would link the same supplier across documents, which is the step that makes
per-supplier aggregation possible and is also the step most exposed to extraction error --- two
spellings of one company name, or one misread tax identifier, split a supplier into two
entities and silently corrupt every aggregate it appears in.

On top of that structure, compliance properties would be derived per document instead of
stored: whether the invoice carries every particular that \S14 \ac{UStG} requires, whether the
stated tax treatment is coherent with the parties' registered identifiers, and whether the
input tax is therefore deductible. Deriving instead of storing keeps a single
authority for each judgement --- the rule, applied to the current record --- so that a
correction to an extracted field propagates to every conclusion drawn from it.

\subsection{The measurement question this layer raises}

The interesting question about this layer is not whether it can be built. It is how error
propagates into it, and that question needs its own apparatus: extraction quality is measured
per field, whereas graph quality has to be assessed at two distinct levels --- whether
individual edges are correct, and whether the resulting structure has the right global
properties \parencite{cai2025}. A graph can score well edge-by-edge and still be wrong in the way
that matters, if a single entity-resolution failure fragments a supplier. Building the layer
without that apparatus would repeat, one level up, exactly the mistake that
Chapter~\ref{ch:measurement-validity} documents at this level.

\section{Layer 3 (Design Only): Analytical Query}
\label{sec:design-layer3}

\emph{Not implemented or evaluated within the scope of this thesis. See
\S\ref{sec:design-scope} and Chapter~\ref{ch:limitations-future-work}.}

The third layer would answer practitioner questions posed in natural language over the graph,
locally, with the same confidentiality constraint as the layers below it.

Two design commitments would govern it. First, \emph{every answer carries its provenance}:
the documents and the specific fields it was derived from, so that a practitioner can verify a
figure rather than trust it. An unsourced answer is not merely less useful in this domain; it
is unusable, because the professional obligation attaches to the booking and not to the tool.
Second, \emph{retrieval strategy is a question to be measured, not assumed}. Flat vector
retrieval over document text and graph-structured retrieval have different strengths, and the
comparison between them is an active, unsettled research question
\parencite{han2025,edge2024graphrag}. The plausible outcome is that the answer differs by question
class --- single-document lookups favouring flat retrieval, multi-document aggregation
favouring the graph --- which is why the design anticipates routing per question instead of
committing to one mechanism.

Both commitments describe an intended design. Neither was implemented, and neither is claimed.

\section{Scope of This Thesis}
\label{sec:design-scope}

\textbf{Layer~1 was built and measured. Layers~2 and~3 were designed and were neither
implemented nor evaluated.} Everything reported in Chapters~\ref{ch:results}
and~\ref{ch:discussion} concerns Layer~1.

The narrowing was a decision taken during the work, and the reason is a finding, not a
shortage of effort.

The measurement of Layer~1 established two things. The first is that the reading stage, not the
structuring stage, is what limits this pipeline (\S\ref{sec:results-attribution}) --- so the
most valuable available work was on the foundation, not on new floors above it. The second is
more uncomfortable and more decisive: the apparatus measuring Layer~1 was itself defective in
several distinct ways, and most of what initially looked like model error was measurement error.
Chapter~\ref{ch:measurement-validity} is the record of finding and correcting those defects.

Given that, building a graph layer on top would have meant constructing a second measurement
problem --- how graph error relates to field error --- on top of a first one that had just been
shown to be unreliable. A wrong answer at the query layer would have been unattributable: it
could have come from the query, the graph, the extraction, or the ruler. The judgement was that
a narrower claim that can be trusted is worth more than a three-layer demonstration that cannot
be interrogated. Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion} returns to this trade-off and to what is lost by it.

Two consequences are recorded, not glossed.

\textbf{The hypotheses covering the upper layers were registered before the narrowing, and are
reported as not evaluated.} They are listed in \S\ref{sec:unevaluated}, and the verbatim
register with registration dates is Appendix~\ref{app:hypotheses}. None of them is dropped
from the record, for the reason Chapter~\ref{ch:limitations-future-work} states.

\textbf{The unbuilt layers are not counted as contributions.} The contribution claimed in
\S\ref{sec:contributions} is Layer~1 together with the measurement methodology that made its
results trustworthy. This chapter describes the full design because the design motivates the
architecture of Layer~1 and because the upper layers are where the work continues --- not
because a diagram is evidence.
